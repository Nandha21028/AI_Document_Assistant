The Midnight Archive

On 17 August 2026, at exactly 10:40 PM, the lights inside the old Meridian Research Archive suddenly went out.

The archive was a small two-floor building located behind the main research laboratory. Only four people were officially authorized to enter the building after 8:00 PM: Dr. Arjun Mehta, Priya Nair, Daniel Joseph, and the security officer, Ravi.

At 10:42 PM, Ravi noticed that the archive's main door was still locked from the inside. He used his security key to open it.

Inside, everything appeared normal except for one thing: the blue metal cabinet in Room 204 was open.

The cabinet was supposed to contain three prototype storage drives:

Drive A — marked ORION-17
Drive B — marked VEGA-09
Drive C — marked LUNA-31

Only Drive B was missing.

Ravi immediately checked the security camera system.

The cameras showed something unusual.

At 9:56 PM, Priya entered the archive carrying a black notebook.

At 10:03 PM, Daniel entered carrying nothing.

At 10:11 PM, Dr. Arjun entered Room 204.

At 10:18 PM, Daniel left the building.

At 10:21 PM, Priya left the building.

At 10:27 PM, Dr. Arjun left the building.

However, the archive's electronic access log showed that someone opened the blue cabinet at 10:31 PM.

This was impossible because the camera showed nobody entering Room 204 after Dr. Arjun left at 10:27 PM.

Ravi initially suspected a malfunction in the access-control system.

He contacted Priya.

Priya said she had entered the archive because Dr. Arjun had asked her to retrieve a document from Room 201. She insisted that she never entered Room 204.

Daniel gave a different explanation.

He said he had entered the archive to return a laboratory tablet that he had accidentally taken home the previous evening. According to Daniel, he left at 10:18 PM because he had finished returning the tablet.

Dr. Arjun said he had entered Room 204 to inspect Drive A.

When asked whether he had touched Drive B, he replied:

"I saw all three drives inside the cabinet. I only checked the label on Drive A."

At 11:05 PM, Ravi discovered another clue.

A small piece of blue plastic was found underneath the table in Room 204.

The plastic was the same color as the protective casing used on Drive B.

However, the plastic did not prove that Drive B had been removed that night. The casing had already been damaged two weeks earlier during a failed experiment.

The investigators then checked the archive's environmental monitoring system.

At 10:29 PM, the temperature inside Room 204 increased from 21.2°C to 24.8°C for exactly three minutes.

The heating system was not scheduled to activate.

This was strange because Drive B was known to generate a small amount of heat when operating.

At 11:32 PM, Ravi checked the network logs.

Drive B had connected to the archive's internal network at 10:30 PM.

Its connection lasted exactly 47 seconds.

The network address assigned to Drive B was 192.168.4.31.

The archive's network administrator later confirmed that this address had previously been assigned to a testing laptop.

That laptop was officially registered to Daniel.

Daniel denied using the laptop that night.

He claimed that the laptop had been left inside Laboratory 3.

Laboratory 3 was approximately 80 meters from the archive.

At midnight, Ravi checked Laboratory 3.

The laptop was there.

Its battery was completely drained.

The laptop's last recorded activity was at 10:29 PM.

One file had been opened on the laptop:

vega_transfer.log

The log contained only three lines:

21:58 - device detected

22:30 - transfer initiated

22:31 - transfer completed

The log did not identify who initiated the transfer.

The next morning, investigators discovered that Drive B had not actually been taken out of the building.

Instead, a hidden compartment beneath the archive's old staircase contained a small wireless storage receiver.

The receiver contained a copy of the data from Drive B.

This created a new mystery.

Someone had copied the contents of Drive B without physically stealing the drive.

The investigators concluded that the person responsible most likely knew:

where Drive B was stored,
how the archive's network was configured,
which laptop could communicate with the drive,
and when the security cameras would not immediately reveal the transfer.

But there was still one problem.

The archive's camera system showed nobody near the staircase between 10:20 PM and 10:40 PM.

At 8:00 AM the following morning, Priya returned to the archive.

She immediately noticed something unusual.

Her black notebook was missing.

She had left it on her desk in Room 201 the previous evening.

Inside that notebook was a handwritten network diagram of the archive.

The diagram included the exact address:

192.168.4.31

Priya later admitted that she had drawn the diagram herself.

However, she insisted that she had never shared it with Daniel.

Daniel claimed he had never seen the notebook.

Dr. Arjun said he had seen Priya's notebook several times but did not remember seeing the network diagram.

Ravi then reviewed the security footage again.

At 10:07 PM, while Priya was supposedly retrieving a document from Room 201, she briefly walked past the door of Room 204.

She did not enter the room.

But she stopped for approximately seven seconds.

The investigation remained unresolved.

There was enough evidence to suspect Daniel.

There was enough evidence to suspect Priya.

There was also evidence suggesting that Dr. Arjun knew more than he admitted.

But none of the evidence conclusively proved who copied Drive B.

The final report therefore classified the incident as:

Unauthorized Data Transfer — Suspect Unknown
